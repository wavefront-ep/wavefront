Wavefront: instructor quick-guide

A 3D heart for teaching how the heartbeat spreads and how arrhythmias arise. One page.

Before class

  • Open the site in a current Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. The first load downloads about 8 MB.
  • No Wi-Fi in the room? Use the offline file wavefront.html (from npm run build:offline). It opens by double-click and needs no internet.
  • Press P (or the Present button) for the projector view: the top bar and side panel disappear, text gets larger. Esc leaves it.

A 10 to 15 minute route

  1. Guided tour (top bar, step 1). Nine short steps: the heart from the front, inside the chambers, the sinus node, the atria, the AV node, His bundle and bundle branches, Purkinje fibres, the whole beat as a colour map, then a pointer to the side panel. Use Next or the right arrow.
  2. Sinus beat (step 2). One normal beat with a Lead II trace underneath. The numbered circles on the trace match the events on the scrubber. Press Space to play or pause.
  3. Arrhythmias (step 3). Pick a rhythm from the list on the right: normal, impulse formation, conduction block, and re-entry. Each opens paused with its key structures labelled. Press Play, or step with the arrow keys.
  4. Cellular view (step 4). A short list of cases where the cell-level mechanism is the point: the cell types in a normal beat, sinus slowing and speeding, AV node conduction, ectopic and junctional automaticity, long QT with an early afterdepolarisation, and the excitable gap in scar-related VT. A strip under the ECG shows the action potential of the cells at one site, lined up with the wavefront, with the phase under the playhead named. Choose the cell type at the left of the strip; "All" overlays them. The strip can also be turned on from the Arrhythmias list with "Cell view" where a case has one.

Things to know

  • Pause at each step (above the play buttons) holds on every event long enough to read its caption and speeds across quiet stretches. It is on for the arrhythmias and off for the sinus rhythm. Turn it off to let a rhythm run continuously.
  • Speed starts at 0.05× so the sequence is easy to follow. 0.02× to 1× are in the bar. The ECG on the strip is on the same time axis as the scrubber.
  • Normal / This rhythm in the player compares an arrhythmia with the normal beat on the same heart.
  • The side panel (Layers and views) has the cutaway, layers (outer wall opacity, conduction system, labels) and standard views. The cut can show the conduction system inside the four-chamber view.

What it is and is not

  • A schematic teaching tool. The action potentials are drawn shapes with textbook-level voltages and durations, timed by the same activation the wavefront uses; they are not a simulation of ion channels. Activation timing is tuned to the teaching points (for example the AV delay, flutter cycle length and re-entry period), but it is not a biophysical simulation or patient data. The conduction pathways other than the main nodes and bundles, the ECG, the re-entry circuits and fibrillation are drawn as schematic and labelled that way in the interface.
  • Heart shape: Rodero et al. 2021, average of a statistical shape model of healthy adult hearts (CC BY 4.0). Valves are drawn as closed planes, not leaflets.
  • Report anything that looks anatomically or physiologically wrong so it can be checked against a reference and corrected.
